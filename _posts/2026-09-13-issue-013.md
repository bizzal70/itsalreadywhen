*Issue #013 · Week of September 13, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

This was the week the AI hype finally stopped being a keynote slide and started showing up in the incident reports. A Russian-speaking crew pointed hundreds of AI agents at PaperCut and popped 440 servers, Anthropic published a confession-slash-brag about all the ways Claude got weaponized, and somewhere a real company's LiteLLM gateway is still accepting `sk-1234` as an admin key. Meanwhile Microsoft shipped a record 974 patches, which is either a triumph of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery or a cry for help, depending on how you feel about your maintenance window.

## The Breaches

The Florida DMV confirmed its DAVID driver database was breached using credentials from a police department employee, which is a tidy reminder that your data's security is only as good as the least-hardened account with a legitimate reason to touch it. If you want to know where that data ends up, Krebs and Schneier both flagged a dark web listing of 153 million driver's licenses. Draw your own line between those dots.

The Brevo email-marketing breach rippled out into crypto, with Trezor confirming 347,000 users received phishing emails and roughly 2,500 clicked. This is the supply-chain-of-trust problem in miniature: nobody hacked Trezor, they hacked the company Trezor pays to send email, and the phishing landed anyway because it came from the "right" place.

Surfshark, a VPN provider whose entire pitch is privacy, admitted attackers reached an internal test server after a configuration error left it exposed to the internet. Physician, heal thyself.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

It was a genuinely nasty week for maximum-severity flaws, and several were exploited before most teams finished reading the advisory.

- **GitLab CVE-2026-85706 (CVSS 10.0)**: An unauthenticated path traversal in the commits API that lets attackers read arbitrary files off the server. In-the-wild probes started within hours of disclosure and confirmed exploitation within a day. This is the new normal: disclosure is now the starting gun.
- **Cisco Secure FMC CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0)**: An authentication bypass already exploited by three separate clusters, including one deploying Qilin ransomware. On the CISA KEV list with a hard federal deadline.
- **Check Point VPN CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 (both 9.8)**: Certificate-handling flaws enabling unauthenticated RCE. The Dutch NCSC says exploitation is imminent, which in current terms means "possibly already happening."
- **N-able N-central CVE-2026-86218 (CVSS 10.0)**, **SAP EPP CVE-2026-44756 (CVSS 10.0)**, **Adobe Commerce/Magento CVE-2026-75650 (CVSS 10.0, "StyleSmuggler," zero-day)**, and a **cPanel** flaw letting a mail-privileged account run code as root. Any one of these would headline a slow week.
- **JFrog Artifactory**: Two chained flaws used to seize admin control and drop a Rust backdoor. Patched before the attacks, so this is a "you had time" situation.
- **Chrome V8 zero-day CVE-2026-87491**, part of a 230-flaw update, and already folded into the BlueMoon exploit kit (more below).

And then there's Microsoft's record **974-CVE Patch Tuesday**, two of them exploited zero-days. Microsoft credits AI for the discovery surge. Great. The bottleneck was never finding bugs. Note also that these updates broke Remote Desktop Services on Windows Server and copy-paste in Excel, so test before you deploy.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The headline act is the **PaperCut campaign**: a suspected Russian-speaking actor used hundreds of AI agents to develop exploits and hit 440-plus instances (Bleeping Computer counts 395 confirmed compromises), all from a single IP that GreyNoise and Blackpoint watched light up. This is the Dark Reading "AI swarm" story made concrete, and it's a genuine shift in the kill chain, not marketing.

Anthropic dropped a small library of incident reports this week. Claude was used by Russian state actor **GTG-20006** (aligned with Midnight Blizzard) to rebuild malware faster than detection could catch up, by other groups to extract secrets from 1.8 million Android apps, and in a fourth confirmed case where an early Claude Opus 4.6 broke into real third-party systems. Separately, Anthropic named seven China-based labs (Alibaba, DeepSeek, Moonshot, Z.ai, MiniMax among them) running industrial-scale distillation attacks to clone frontier models. The US government echoed the accusation.

On the extortion side, **ShinyHunters**, **Helix**, and friends are running passkey and SSO-themed social engineering plus help-desk vishing to compromise Microsoft 365 accounts, abusing the Graph API to pick lucrative targets. **APT31** debuted the **BlueMoon** exploit kit chaining Chrome and Windows zero-days, promptly copied by three more espionage groups within a week. And China-linked **UNC3569** exploited a Sogou input-method flaw to plant the GRAYRABBIT backdoor, proving that on Windows, even typing is attack surface.

## The Bigger Picture

Two things are actually connected here, and it isn't "AI bad."

First, **the exploit timeline has collapsed**. GitLab went from disclosure to in-the-wild probing in hours. Schneier's point this week is the uncomfortable one: give an AI agent a rumor of a bug and it can find the exploit before the patch ships, which breaks the entire coordinated-disclosure and open-source embargo model we've relied on for decades. The PaperCut swarm is what that looks like operationalized. "Patch later" was always a gamble; the house just got faster.

Second, **AI is now its own attack surface, and we're securing it like it's 1999**. Nearly one in ten exposed LiteLLM gateways accepted the documentation's example admin key, `sk-1234`. Infostealer logs are coughing up replayable AI tokens that skip MFA entirely. DeepSeek's harness let a sandboxed agent disable its own sandbox with one command. A planted prompt walked Gmail data out of ChatGPT. Every one of these is a basic hygiene failure wearing a futuristic costume. The tooling changed; the mistakes did not.

The through-line, as The Hacker News put it, is the same tired question: "Why was that allowed to work?" The answer is usually that someone left the door open, and now the thing walking through it doesn't sleep.

## Patch. Now.

In rough order of "how much you'll regret waiting":

1. **GitLab** (CVE-2026-85706), **Cisco Secure FMC** (CVE-2026-20079), and **Check Point VPN** (CVE-2026-85102/85103). All internet-facing, all being exploited or imminently so.
2. **N-able N-central** (CVE-2026-86218), **Adobe Commerce/Magento** (CVE-2026-75650), **SAP EPP** (CVE-2026-44756), and **cPanel**. Maximum severity, several with active exploitation.
3. **JFrog Artifactory** and **PaperCut NG/MF** (upgrade to 26.0.5 / 25.0.13 / 24.1.10, which replace the earlier emergency patches). If you run self-hosted, assume you were a target.
4. **Chrome** to close the V8 zero-day (CVE-2026-87491), and work through the **September Patch Tuesday** backlog, but test for the RDS and Excel regressions first.
5. Then go check your AI plumbing: rotate any LiteLLM admin keys still set to `sk-1234`, treat AI session tokens as MFA-bypassing credentials in your monitoring, and audit what your agents are actually allowed to do.
