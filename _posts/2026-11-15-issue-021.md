*Issue #021 · Week of November 15, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The AI you deployed to make everything faster is now making your breaches faster too. This week gave us stolen keys poisoning official developer tooling, agentic pen-test frameworks running credential attacks at machine speed, and the Pentagon rebranding its AI office to "Super Intelligence" because apparently that is what passes for strategy now. The machines are not going rogue. The machines are just doing exactly what we told them to, quickly, in every direction at once.

## The Breaches

The headline is the supply chain, and the supply chain is AI infrastructure. Cursor confirmed that its official Claude Code extension was trojanized after an attacker stole an access key that **Fireworks AI** left exposed in a breach. Fireworks then confirmed the damage runs deeper than first reported: customer datasets, secrets, incomplete model weights, and Notion AI chats. One exposed key, one poisoned marketplace, every downstream developer who trusted it.

The pattern repeats. **Railway** says stolen customer AI provider keys ran up an estimated $740,000 in unauthorized usage, and to its credit is eating the charges itself. Over **4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts** were used to plant a CI secret stealer across 1,806-plus repositories, harvesting exactly the cloud credentials that keep the whole machine running. **NEAR AI** had an inference server popped via an SGLang RCE, potentially exposing requests sent in "confidential" mode. Notice the theme: the secrets store is the crown jewel, and we have built an entire industry of new secrets stores that nobody has threat-modeled yet.

On the hardware side, **Trezor** confirmed three Safe 5 wallets sold through a Southeast Asian reseller shipped with physical implants that read recovery phrases off the screen and exfiltrated them over cellular. If you bought a hardware wallet from a third party, that is a supply chain too.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

Microsoft's Patch Tuesday was, again, a four-digit affair: **1,259 flaws**, including **CVE-2026-124194**, an actively exploited Outlook bug that can leak NTLMv2 hashes. That one is in the KEV catalog. Patch it before you read the rest of this section.

The HTTP/2 stack had a rough week. **Apache HTTP Server** fixed **CVE-2026-119618**, a critical unauthenticated RCE in mod_http2 (patched in 2.4.71). **Node.js** fixed HTTP/2 flaws that crash servers or leak other connections' memory. If you run anything internet-facing, you run one of these.

Edge and infrastructure gear, as always, is where the real fires are:

- **Check Point** patched a zero-day (**CVE-2026-123039**, now in KEV) exploited since late October for root access, with attackers chaining PHP2Shell to drop the TSGA backdoor and wipe ESXi hosts.
- **Citrix NetScaler** fixed **CVE-2026-120868** (CVSS 9.3) and an auth bypass. You know the drill with NetScaler by now.
- **SAP** shipped four CVSS 10.0 flaws in NetWeaver and Web Dispatcher, two unauthenticated RCEs.
- **N-able N-central** is a disaster in progress: 33 flaws, public exploits ("4EverAble2Shell," "Agent2Shell") that work against *patched* servers, and the company pulling features offline. If you run on-prem N-central, assume compromise and verify.
- **Zoom Workplace** (**CVE-2026-108186**, CVSS 9.6), **Red Hat Quay**, **PTC Windchill** (**CVE-2026-126325**), and **MongoDB** (**CVE-2026-118383**) all shipped critical fixes.

Two architectural ones worth noting: the **Mior and Skyfall** attacks break AMD SEV-SNP confidential VMs from a malicious host, and Microsoft researchers showed you can **backdoor an AI model by editing fewer than 10 neurons**. Both require privileged access, but both quietly undermine the "trust the hardware" and "trust the model" assumptions everyone is building on.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The state actors stayed busy. The **FBI** disrupted the DPRK's **FaceStealer** campaign, which used deepfake video calls to steal over $400 million in crypto across 18 months. **SilentAir**, assessed as state-level, hijacked equipment inside at least six Asian telecom networks to intercept calls and SMS 2FA codes, mostly targeting Taiwanese users. Russia's **Turla** is running a new modular toolset called **Cereal** to quietly loot government Exchange servers.

On the criminal side, Krebs put a name and a St. Petersburg address to the administrator of **Qilin**, now the most active ransomware affiliate program with 365 victims in three months. **Luna Moth** is back to re-extort law firms that refused to pay the first time. And **Operation Endgame** took down Gootloader, CastleRAT, and StealC infrastructure, seizing 6.4 million stolen logins, while a separate operation arrested 354 dark web marketplace users. Good weeks for the good guys, for once.

The genuinely new thing is the arrival of weaponized agentic pen-test tools. **AntiFox** and **Artex**, framed as legitimate security tooling, are now being used to steal AWS production secrets and move laterally "at machine speed." A Dark Reading piece documented a US retailer breach where the attacker, after hand-typed commands failed, switched to AI-generated scripts and achieved lateral movement in minutes. This is no longer theoretical.

## The Bigger Picture

Two threads tie this week together, and both run through AI.

First, we have invented an enormous new attack surface and called it progress. AI inference servers, model marketplaces, agent identities, API key vaults, Grok-built apps (**35,578** of which Claroc found exposed online with no sign-in) are all shiny new systems holding highly valuable secrets, deployed faster than anyone can secure them. The Fireworks-to-Cursor chain is the template for the next five years: compromise the AI infrastructure provider, poison everyone downstream.

Second, and more telling, **Google Project Zero** quietly changed its disclosure policy because AI has compressed the window between a bug being reported and being exploited. When the people who set the industry's patching clock say the clock is now too slow, that is not hype. That is the defenders admitting the tempo has fundamentally shifted. Combine faster discovery, faster exploitation, and agentic tooling that executes at machine speed, and the comfortable assumption that you have 90 days to patch is dead. The Pentagon can call it "Super Intelligence" all it likes. The rest of us have to defend against it on Tuesday.

## Patch. Now.

In rough order of how much you should panic:

1. **Microsoft Outlook (CVE-2026-124194)** — actively exploited, in KEV, leaks NTLMv2 hashes. Deploy the November updates, and check Office updates separately.
2. **Check Point Security Gateways (CVE-2026-123039)** — exploited since October for root; apply the emergency hotfixes and hunt for TSGA and ESXi tampering.
3. **N-able N-central** — if on-prem, assume compromise, disable the Legacy API, pull it off the public internet, and verify against the live exploits.
4. **Apache HTTP Server (CVE-2026-119618)** and **Node.js** HTTP/2 — upgrade to 2.4.71 / 22.23.5 / 24.22.3 / 26.13.1 if internet-facing.
5. **SAP NetWeaver / Web Dispatcher** — four CVSS 10.0 flaws; patch the unauthenticated RCEs immediately.
6. **Citrix NetScaler (CVE-2026-120868)**, **Zoom Workplace (CVE-2026-108186)**, and the KEV additions (**Cisco BroadWorks CVE-2026-20320**, **Barracuda**, **UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713**) — work them in this week.
7. **Rotate your AI provider keys**, audit where your secrets actually live, and give your AI agents their own scoped identities before someone else does it for you.
