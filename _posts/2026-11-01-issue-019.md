*Issue #019 · Week of November 01, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

If you held cryptocurrency, trusted your firewall, or installed a code editor extension this week, congratulations: you were a target. The headline number is $6.43 billion walking out of Binance because a DevOps engineer opened the wrong thing on his laptop, but the quieter story is that AI models are now writing working exploits faster than anyone can patch them, and the industry's response is to sell you more AI. We are, as ever, exactly where you'd expect.

## The Breaches

The record books have a new entry. The FBI has attributed the theft of more than $6.43 billion in virtual assets from Binance to North Korea's **TraderTraitor** (also Jade Sleet, UNC4899, and a half-dozen other names). Binance traced the compromise to a single DevOps engineer's laptop. Nearly $3 billion has been frozen on-chain, Tether and Circle clawed back another 2.3 billion between them, and the rest is somewhere in Pyongyang's balance sheet. The lesson, if anyone still needs it: the world's largest exchange has the same attack surface as everyone else, which is to say, a human being with a computer.

Crypto was the theme. **Polymarket** lost about $2.7 million when attackers swapped out a page-speed script loaded from third party DebugBear, hijacked signed-in sessions, and drained wallets. A textbook supply chain attack through a dependency nobody thinks about. Meanwhile **Trezor** is warning that a Safe 5 hardware wallet sold through a Southeast Asian reseller shipped with an extra circuit board that quietly broadcasts your recovery phrase over a cellular network. If you bought a Trezor from CryptoBilis since March, assume your funds are gone and start over.

Elsewhere, **Chainalysis** disclosed a breach via what it delicately called "offensive agentic techniques," exposing customer names, emails, and org roles. Blockchain analytics firm, breached by AI. There is poetry in that. And 41,000 Homeland Security workers had their contact details and internal group memberships published, in case you thought the government was faring better.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

Plenty to choose from, so let's stick to what is actually being exploited.

**GitLab CVE-2026-97229** (CVSS 10.0) is an integer overflow allowing unauthenticated remote code execution via a crafted CI regex. It is in CISA's KEV catalog and attackers already took a swing at Alpine Linux's own GitLab server. Patch it.

**Ivanti Connect Secure** continues its tradition. CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527 are being chained for unauthenticated root access and are now in KEV. If you still run internet-facing Ivanti, you know the drill by now, or you should.

The **PHP2Shell** flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) are being used to root Palo Alto firewall management interfaces exposed to the internet. Over 22 million instances are theoretically in scope. Your firewall management interface should not be on the public internet. It is 2026.

Two things stand out because they are old and everywhere. A **16-year-old UEFI flaw in EDK II** lets code run before Secure Boot on network-booting servers. And **Drupal Commerce Core** (CVE-2026-118539) is under active exploitation against online stores through an authorization bypass. Also worth your time: Cisco ASA/FTD **CVE-2026-76418** (exploited, in KEV), the Linux MPTCP kernel flaw **CVE-2026-98432** (CVSS 9.8, remote root), and Adobe Commerce PolyShell activity hitting hundreds of stores.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

North Korea's TraderTraitor owns the week, but the China-nexus crews were busy. **Volt Typhoon** was running a covert access network dubbed Silent Train, built from compromised SOHO and IoT gear, used for staging zero-day campaigns. **Warp Panda** exploited a VMware ESX zero-day (CVE-2026-41709) specifically to reduce audit trail visibility while targeting the US legal sector, which tells you they are thinking about forensics, not just access. And **Vapor Panda** was caught using a Go-based AI-powered pentest framework called ARTEX against 200-plus organizations.

On the criminal side, **ShinyHunters** have been riding a chained Oracle PeopleSoft exploit (CVE-2026-35273) since May. A trojanized **Claude Code** extension slipped into the Cursor marketplace for eleven hours and tried to plant SSH backdoors on up to 5,000 developer machines. And law enforcement had a good week for once: Interpol's Operation Serengeti logged 1,462 arrests, the FBI seized the Virtualine bulletproof host, and a Syrian Electronic Army member finally got 42 months.

## The Bigger Picture

The through-line this week is AI crossing from talking point to tooling, on both sides of the fence.

Anthropic's unreleased model chained real vulnerabilities into working proof-of-concept exploits across Chrome, Safari, Windows, Android, and Linux. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon is topping vulnerability-discovery benchmarks. Project Zero found its LLM BigSleep outfinding a traditional fuzzer, 45 bugs to 37. These are defensive framings, but the capability is neutral: an exploit generated to be patched is the same exploit generated to be fired. The Linux MPTCP root exploit this week came out of Anthropic's lab. The window between disclosure and weaponization is now measured in hours, which is precisely why that GitLab bug got swung at Alpine a week after the fix.

And the attackers have noticed. ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI's 44 agents against Japan, Chainalysis breached by "offensive agentic techniques," phishing kits impersonating AI bots to scrape credentials. Meanwhile the research corner offers two genuinely unsettling papers: AI models can provably hide arbitrary capabilities in their weights, and Qwen3-Coder writes measurably weaker code when you tell it the customer is American. The supply chain now includes the model itself. Sleep well.

## Patch. Now.

In rough order of how much it should ruin your week:

- **GitLab CVE-2026-97229** (CVSS 10.0, exploited). Patch immediately. It is in KEV and actively targeted.
- **Ivanti Connect Secure** CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527 (chained for root, in KEV). Patch or pull it offline.
- **PHP / PHP2Shell** CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. Patch, and get your Palo Alto and firewall management interfaces off the public internet.
- **Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418** (exploited, in KEV) and **Drupal Commerce CVE-2026-118539** (exploited against stores).
- **Linux kernel MPTCP CVE-2026-98432** (CVSS 9.8) and the **TCP flaw CVE-2026-98432** across Ubuntu/RHEL. Update your distros.
- **Oracle E-Business Suite CVE-2026-101767** (CVSS 9.8) now being probed, days after patch.
- Rotate anything stored on **Northflank**, and rotate the 26,400 cloud/AI credentials sitting in public Gitee repos if any are yours.
- Audit your IDE extensions. If you pulled a Claude Code update on Cursor around October 29, rebuild the machine.
