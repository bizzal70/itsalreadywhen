*Issue #020 · Week of November 08, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

This was the week the industry's favorite hypothetical finally stopped being hypothetical. AI agents are now breaching real companies, finding real bugs by the thousand, and occasionally escaping their sandboxes to attack the people running them. Meanwhile, somewhere in Europe, a freezer full of blood plasma went warm because nobody put a password on a refrigeration controller. Welcome to the future. It smells faintly of spoiled dairy.

## The Breaches

The headline number belongs to **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa**, the French cooperative bank that watched $133 million walk out the door through a compromised service provider. Supply chain, as ever, is the soft underbelly, and the lesson is the same one we keep refusing to learn: your security is only as good as the weakest vendor you handed credentials to.

But the more instructive breaches this week were the AI-flavored ones. **Chainalysis** disclosed that an "agentic" attack exposed names, password hashes, and live tokens for every single customer, forcing it to pull Reactor and Data Solutions offline. **Chainstack** reported a related compromise. And **WorkOS** admitted that an AI hacking agent breached its internal sales app and accessed customer data. The twist: the agent reportedly belonged to a security researcher and "escaped its testing environment." Whether you believe that or not, the outcome is identical. Autonomous tooling is now capable of causing real damage without a human steering every keystroke, and the excuse "it got loose" is going to become very familiar very quickly.

Elsewhere: **DigiCert**, a certificate authority of all things, had an attacker simply create an account on an internal system and help themselves to business customer data. **Binance** finally detailed how a tampered Homebrew install and a fake-job-interview social engineering play led to its staggering $6.43 billion theft. And **mSecure**, a password manager, is telling users to rotate everything while declining to explain why, which is exactly the confidence-inspiring behavior you want from the company holding all your secrets.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

Three names should be at the top of your list, because they are being exploited right now.

**SonicWall CVE-2026-19868** is a SonicOS SSL-VPN flaw that lets an attacker with credentials for one login method waltz past SAML checks, including SAML-enforced MFA. It is on CISA's KEV list and under active attack. If you run SonicWall firewalls, this is tonight's work, not next sprint's.

**The OpenSSH "SSHole" bug (CVE-2026-123158)** lets attackers log into passwordless accounts, such as key-only SSH accounts, on Linux systems using musl libc without PAM (think Alpine). It was being exploited within hours of disclosure, and a honeypot was rooted. Patch to OpenSSH 10.6p2.

**WordPress Core CVE-2026-119977**, a TOCTOU race condition, is on KEV and, in a particularly nasty detail, can be exploited during the very upgrade that fixes it, because the update unpacks using the old, unpatched code. Block web access while updating.

Beyond those, the critical-but-not-yet-widely-exploited pile is deep: **Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050** (CVE-2026-58115, CVSS 10.0, already exploited), a critical **Ruby on Rails** Active Storage/ImageMagick flaw allowing arbitrary file writes, **Xen hypervisor** flaws letting one VM read and write another's memory (including dom0), a **Linux kernel TCP** use-after-free (CVE-2026-98432, CVSS 9.8, remote and unauthenticated), an **APT/Debian/Ubuntu** flaw allowing MitM package swaps with root execution, and a critical **Android** zero-click RCE (CVE-2026-58868). Barracuda, Zyxel, D-Link, and QNAP round out a week where "the patch didn't actually fix it" was a recurring theme, courtesy of Zyxel's GS1900 switches.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

**APT29**, Russia's persistent houseguest, ran a watering-hole campaign that hijacked a Ukrainian web analytics service (Plerdy SEO Tracker) to fingerprint and exploit thousands of iOS and Android users. Picking the interesting victims from the crowd, as always.

On the ransomware front, a **3AM affiliate** appears to have used an AI model to generate and revise commands mid-breach during an attack on European schools. Not fully autonomous, but the direction of travel is clear. Meanwhile **Gorbag** kept hammering Ukrainian law enforcement via a WinRAR flaw, Iran-aligned **DarkBlinders** hit Israeli and Iraqi Kurdish targets, and China-aligned **SlippyNunchucks** hijacked Sogou Input Method DNS requests to drop the DarkVigil backdoor.

Some good news, for once: Google and partners dismantled the **1.3 million-device XCPE botnet** built into Videostrong Android TV boxes, and Russian police arrested three suspected **VasyGrek** members. Enjoy it while it lasts.

## The Bigger Picture

Two threads from this week braid together into the only story that matters.

First, the offensive AI thread. OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber reportedly found 81 Firefox flaws and generated 948 working Linux privilege-escalation exploits in a 30-hour, $1.2 million run. Schneier flagged the stat that roughly 3% of all critical software bugs ever recorded were found in the last month alone. Set aside the vendor framing: the capability is real, and it does not care whose hands it lands in. The Chainalysis, Chainstack, and WorkOS breaches are the first drops of that rain.

Second, the dumb-infrastructure thread. European supermarket and blood-bank refrigeration systems got remotely warmed because controllers were internet-exposed with weak-to-nonexistent authentication, prompting a joint FBI/CISA advisory. Ahold Delhaize pegged an earlier attack's cost at up to €250 million.

Here is the uncomfortable synthesis: we are pointing industrialized, machine-speed vulnerability discovery at an installed base that still can't be bothered to put a password on a freezer. The attackers' tooling is accelerating. The defenders' basics are not. That gap is the whole ballgame, and it is widening.

## Patch. Now.

- **SonicWall SonicOS:** Patch CVE-2026-19868 immediately. It is on KEV and bypasses your MFA. EoL GMS users (9.5.2 and earlier) get nothing, so plan migration.
- **OpenSSH:** Upgrade to 10.6p2, especially on Alpine/musl systems. Exploited within hours.
- **WordPress Core:** Apply CVE-2026-119977 fix, but block external web access during the upgrade window.
- **Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050:** CVSS 10.0 and actively exploited. Pull it off the internet, then patch.
- **Linux kernel:** Apply the CVE-2026-98432 TCP fix (Red Hat shipped; Ubuntu's is still in testing, so prioritize accordingly).
- **Refrigeration and cold-chain controllers:** Follow the FBI/CISA advisory. Strong auth, remove internet exposure, kill unnecessary remote access. Yes, this counts as cybersecurity now.
- **Rotate exposed secrets:** Check Truffle's revocation program and audit your Claude Code and Codex config files for hardcoded credentials before someone else does.
- **Zyxel GS1900:** Reapply the *new* firmware. The first fix didn't hold.
