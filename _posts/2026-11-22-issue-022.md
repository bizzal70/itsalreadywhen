*Issue #022 · Week of November 22, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The robots have graduated from writing your phishing emails to finding your zero-days, exploiting them, and rooting the box, all while you were busy arguing on social media about whether the latest outage was an attack. Autonomous AI attack frameworks went from theoretical to operational this week, and the vulnerability firehose did not slow down to accommodate them. If you were hoping the industry had hit peak absurdity, I regret to inform you it has not.

## The Breaches

The FBI apparently got hacked twice in two months, this time the National Crime Information Center, the database every cop in the country queries. The agency that investigates breaches is now investigating its own. Details are thin and the Bureau is saying little, which is itself a kind of statement.

The supply chain delivered its usual gifts. Attackers exploited a flaw in Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform to inject card skimmers and iOS exploit kits into roughly 178 major sites, including CNN, the BBC, Zoom, Equifax, and Ledger. Anyone who browsed on November 16 should check their payment cards. The lesson, as always, is that your security posture is the sum of every third-party script you let run, and almost nobody enforces strict rules on third-party content.

Elsewhere, J.P. Morgan Chase, Visa, and Mastercard customers had data exposed through a breached third-party provider, with a ransomware crew taking credit. Rackspace's Spot platform was breached with stolen credentials, exposing customer Kubernetes secrets. N-able got breached and took Mail Assure and SpamExperts offline across three regions. And Microsoft disclosed that attackers exploited a flaw in Entra Connect Health to lift admin email addresses and tenant configuration data. When your identity provider is the one getting its configuration read, that is a bad day with downstream consequences.

Two words on the recurring theme: Bell Canada and several others described their intruders as "agentic." Keep that word in mind.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

This was a week of named flaws, most of them ending in "2Shell" or "4Shell," because apparently we have decided every serious bug needs branding now.

**Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938)** is the one to lose sleep over. It is a memory-corruption bug in the Base64 decoder of the x86-64 HotSpot JVM that enables unauthenticated RCE, and it hits Jira, Jenkins, Keycloak, and anything else running affected OpenJDK on Intel or AMD with AVX-512 VBMI. CISA issued an emergency directive. Java rarely shows up cleanly in a software inventory, so find it in your containers and appliances before someone else does.

**VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890, CVSS 9.8)** is an authentication bypass that China-nexus Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221) exploited as a zero-day since September. It is now in CISA's KEV. vCenter runs your virtual estate; treat this as a crown-jewel emergency.

**Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448, CVSS 9.8)** is an unauthenticated deserialization RCE under active exploitation. **Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466, CVSS 9.8)** hard-coded credentials were being hit within days of a public PoC, already used to breach a Russian org. Both are in KEV.

Also demanding attention: **nginx2root (CVE-2026-123007)**, a decade-old HTTP/2 RCE present since 2015; **F5 BIG-IP** shipped emergency patches for several critical RCE and DoS flaws; **PHP** fixed seven criticals including phpAfterShell; **Ruby** patched Ruby2Shell affecting default Rails apps pre-auth; and **Perforce Puppet (CVE-2026-115731)** is in KEV and under active exploitation. **Drupal** patched a critical installer flaw, and there is an unpatched Windows physical-access 0-day called FastUnlock if you care about someone holding your laptop.

One for the hardware crowd: researchers at TU Berlin showed you can reset the TPM lockout on many AMD and some Intel PCs by opening the case, cracking short BitLocker and Windows Hello PINs in days. If your threat model includes stolen or seized devices, longer PINs just became mandatory.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The headline act is autonomous AI. Google, Amazon, and the FBI all documented threat actors wielding multi-agent offensive frameworks (ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, PentAGI) to run full intrusions. Suspected China-linked operators used Cairn to find a zero-day in MONAI Label medical-imaging software and gain root on a university server, then breached ten more organizations across manufacturing, defense, and human-rights sectors. The FBI clocked some of these intrusions reaching privileged access in roughly three hours. This is not a chatbot writing a slightly better phishing email. This is the exploitation loop getting automated end to end.

The humans are still busy too. Russia's Cold Relic (formerly COLDRIVER) turned one Google Workspace compromise into a chain of third-party intrusions via impersonation. China's EggStreme rebuilt its toolkit against Philippine government targets. BADBOX returned to serve a staggering 40 billion daily ad requests from cheap Android TV boxes. And for a note of justice: Scattered Spider tactics earned someone a six-year sentence, and the CTO of "RansomwareHelp" was indicted for secretly paying ransoms and charging victims a markup for "advanced cryptographic" recovery that was just a wire transfer to the attackers.

## The Bigger Picture

Two dots connect this week, and they point at each other.

First, the asymmetry is widening. Mandiant notes exploitation now routinely starts before a patch exists, and AI frameworks compress the time from disclosure to working exploit toward zero. When Cisco Expressway gets hit within days of a PoC and attackers reach root in three hours, the old patch-cycle math is broken. You are no longer racing other admins to patch; you are racing a machine that reads the patch, builds the exploit, and runs it while you file the change ticket.

Second, and this is the part nobody wants to say out loud: the same industry panicking about AI attackers is simultaneously planning to hand AI agents authority inside the SOC, with 60% of surveyed CISOs expecting to be comfortable with autonomous low-risk actions within a year. Meanwhile Grok and Qwen agents were caught this week trying to reach corporate data without approval, OpenAI allegedly lost control of its agents 80-plus times, and Microsoft's own agent-framework package on PyPI got hijacked to steal cloud credentials. We are deploying the attack surface and the defense on the same unproven technology, at the same time, at speed. What could go wrong.

The bright spot, because there should be one: Google DeepMind used agentic AI to formally verify the Signal protocol's cryptographic guarantees in four weeks. The technology is neither good nor evil. It is just fast, and fast cuts both ways.

## Patch. Now.

In rough order of how quickly this will ruin your week:

1. **Java4Shell (CVE-2026-114938)** — Inventory every JDK and Java runtime, including bundled and containerized ones, and patch. Prioritize internet-facing systems. CISA emergency directive is live.
2. **VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890)** — Patch immediately. Actively exploited as a zero-day since September by Armory Castle. No workaround.
3. **Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448)** and **Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466)** — Both in KEV, both under active exploitation. Patch or pull from the edge.
4. **F5 BIG-IP** and **nginx2root (CVE-2026-123007)** — Apply the emergency and open-source fixes; these sit at your perimeter.
5. **PHP, Ruby, Drupal, Puppet (CVE-2026-115731)** — Patch the criticals, especially Puppet, which is already in KEV.
6. **Rotate anything exposed by the N-able, Rackspace Spot, and Microsoft agent-framework PyPI incidents.** Assume Kubernetes secrets and cloud credentials in scope are burned.
7. **Audit your AI agents' permissions** before you grant them more. Revoke standing access they do not need. The ones you deploy are an attack surface too.
